How a home battery saves you money

Savings happen automatically once the battery is configured. Backup during a blackout is a separate design choice.

  1. Daytime

    Your panels make more than the house uses. Without a battery, the surplus is exported for little value.

  2. Stored

    The battery charges from that surplus instead of sending it to the grid.

  3. Evening

    Cooking, lights and appliances run from storage instead of buying grid power.

Backup is designed in, not automatic

Keeping circuits on during a blackout only works where changeover equipment and essential circuits are specifically designed and installed for it. Not every battery installation includes this, so we decide with you which circuits (lighting, fridge, internet) should stay on before the system is designed.

Battery storage questions

Is a battery right for my existing solar?

It depends on how much solar you currently export unused, your evening electricity use, and your existing inverter's compatibility. A short assessment of your bill and system details usually answers this before any site visit.

Will a battery work with my current inverter?

Some inverters have a matching battery from the same manufacturer; others need an AC-coupled battery with its own inverter. We'll confirm what your specific system supports before quoting.

What should I know about backup power?

Most battery systems don't automatically back up your whole home. Backup is usually a defined set of essential circuits, set up deliberately during installation, so it's worth deciding what should stay on before the system is designed.

What size battery do I need?

As a rough guide: around 5 to 7 kWh for a small household using it mainly in the evening, 10 to 13 kWh for a typical family home with some backup circuits, and 13 kWh or more for larger loads or overnight EV charging. Your size is confirmed from your usage during the assessment.

How is the federal battery discount worked out?

The program pays out in small-scale technology certificates (STCs), not a fixed dollar amount. For an eligible battery installed from 1 May to 31 December 2026, the base is 6.8 STCs per kWh of usable capacity, at 100% to 14 kWh, 60% to 28 kWh and 15% to 50 kWh. A 10 kWh battery may create 68 STCs. The dollar value is confirmed in your quote.

How long does installation take?

A straightforward retrofit, where your switchboard already has capacity, is usually completed in a single visit. If backup circuit work or a switchboard upgrade is needed first, that's scoped and quoted separately during your assessment, so there are no surprises on install day.
