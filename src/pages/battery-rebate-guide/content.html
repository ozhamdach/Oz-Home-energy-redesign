The Federal Battery Rebate, Explained

How the Cheaper Home Batteries Program actually reduces what you pay for a home battery — the STC factor, the taper, and a worked example.

What it is

How the federal battery discount is calculated

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program supports eligible battery installations through small-scale technology certificates (STCs). For an eligible battery installed from 1 May to 31 December 2026, the base factor is 6.8 STCs per kWh of usable capacity, subject to the capacity taper below.

Worked example

Worked example: 10 kWh usable battery

A qualifying 10 kWh usable battery installed during this period may create 68 STCs: 10 kWh × 6.8. This is a certificate estimate, not a fixed dollar discount. The dollar value depends on the STC price, the retailer or registered agent's offer, applicable fees and final eligibility.

How the factor tapers

The factor steps down as usable capacity increases

Usable capacityFactor
0 – 14 kWh inclusive100%
Above 14 kWh, up to 28 kWh inclusive60%
Above 28 kWh, up to 50 kWh inclusive15%

The 6.8 STC-per-kWh base factor applies from 1 May to 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027 the base factor is scheduled to step down to 5.7.

Good to know

  • Eligible systems must generally have a nominal capacity between 5 kWh and 100 kWh.
  • STCs can be claimed only for the first 50 kWh of usable capacity.
  • The factor applies at 100% through 14 kWh, 60% for capacity above 14 kWh through 28 kWh, and 15% for capacity above 28 kWh through 50 kWh.

The STC count is not a fixed dollar rebate — the dollar value depends on the STC price, assignment arrangement, fees, product eligibility and installation date, confirmed as part of your quotation. As of 25 Sep 2026. Sources: Clean Energy Regulator — calculate STC entitlements, Clean Energy Regulator — solar batteries.
