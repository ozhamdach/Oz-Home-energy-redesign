How a home battery works

Your panels peak at noon. A battery keeps what the house doesn't use, and your switchboard sends it to the evening.

We check your system first

Already have solar? We look at the brand, age and condition of what's on your wall before recommending a battery. Starting from nothing? Solar and battery can be designed and installed together, and older systems may need solar and battery upgrades first.

  • Inverter check

    Brand, age and warranty status reviewed before we recommend a specific battery.

  • DC or AC coupled

    Some inverters take a matching battery from the same maker; others need an AC-coupled battery with its own inverter.

  • Switchboard capacity

    We confirm there's safe capacity and correct protection for the added equipment.

  • Backup circuits

    You choose which circuits stay on in an outage (lighting, fridge, internet) and we wire backup to suit.

  • Licensed installation

    Work to Australian Standards, backed by our 10-year workmanship warranty. Manufacturer warranties are separate.

  • Commissioning

    System tested and, where applicable, the monitoring app set up so you can see performance.

  • Usually one visit

    A straightforward retrofit, where your switchboard already has capacity, is usually done in a single visit.

  • No surprises

    If backup wiring or a switchboard upgrade is needed first, it's scoped and quoted during your assessment.

  • Ongoing support

    One point of contact for servicing, firmware questions or adding storage later.

What size battery do I need?

A certificate estimate, not a fixed dollar discount. The dollar value depends on the STC price, the retailer or registered agent's offer, fees and final eligibility. Eligible systems generally have a nominal capacity of 5 to 100 kWh, and certificates are claimed on the first 50 kWh of usable capacity. Your actual size comes from your usage, and the final figure is confirmed in your quote. As of 25 September 2026. Sources: Clean Energy Regulator, calculating STC entitlements; Clean Energy Regulator, solar batteries.

Savings first, backup by design

Savings happen automatically once the battery is configured. Backup during a blackout is a separate decision: it only works where changeover equipment and essential circuits are designed and installed for it, and not every battery installation includes it.

  1. Noon

    Your panels make more than the house uses. Without a battery, the surplus is exported for little value.

  2. Stored

    The battery charges from that surplus. On backup systems, chosen circuits are wired to draw from it in an outage.

  3. Evening

    Cooking, lights and appliances run from storage instead of buying power from the grid.

Battery questions

Is a battery right for my existing solar?

It depends on how much solar you currently export unused, your evening electricity use, and your existing inverter's compatibility. A short assessment of your bill and system details usually answers this before any site visit.

Will a battery work with my current inverter?

Some inverters have a matching battery from the same manufacturer; others need an AC-coupled battery with its own inverter. We'll confirm what your specific system supports before quoting.

What should I know about backup power?

Most battery systems don't automatically back up your whole home. Backup is usually a defined set of essential circuits, set up deliberately during installation, so it's worth deciding what should stay on before the system is designed.

What size battery do I need?

As a rough guide: around 5 to 7 kWh for a small household using it mainly in the evening, 10 to 13 kWh for a typical family home with some backup circuits, and 13 kWh or more for larger loads or overnight EV charging. Your size is confirmed from your usage during the assessment.

How is the federal battery discount worked out?

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program pays out in small-scale technology certificates (STCs), not a fixed dollar amount. For an eligible battery installed from 1 May to 31 December 2026, the base is 6.8 STCs per kWh of usable capacity, at 100% to 14 kWh, 60% to 28 kWh and 15% to 50 kWh. A 10 kWh battery may create 68 STCs. The dollar value is confirmed in your quote.

How long does installation take?

A straightforward retrofit, where your switchboard already has capacity, is usually completed in a single visit. If backup circuit work or a switchboard upgrade is needed first, that's scoped and quoted separately during your assessment, so there are no surprises on install day.
