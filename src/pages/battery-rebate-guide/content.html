The Federal Battery Rebate, Explained

How the Cheaper Home Batteries Program actually reduces what you pay for a home battery in Sydney — the rate, the taper, and a worked example.

What it is

A discount applied at the time of purchase, not a separate claim

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program is a federal discount on the upfront cost of an eligible battery system (5-100 kWh usable capacity). Your installer applies it directly to your quote — you don't claim it separately afterwards.

Worked example

10 kWh battery, Sydney (Zone 3)

At the current Zone 3 rate of approximately $252 per usable kWh, a 10 kWh battery (under the 14 kWh full-rate cap) works out to roughly $2,520 off the upfront cost — commonly summarised as around a 30% discount on typical system pricing.

This is an illustration of how the rate applies to one size, not a product this business sells at exactly that size or a quote.

How the rate tapers

The rate steps down as the battery gets bigger

Battery sizeRate
0 – 14 kWhFull rate (100%)
14 – 28 kWh60% of the full rate
28 – 50 kWh15% of the full rate

The per-kWh rate itself also steps down roughly every 6 months until the scheme ends in 2030 — the next change is expected 1 January 2027.

Good to know

  • Eligible battery size range: 5-100 kWh usable capacity.
  • Applied by your installer at time of purchase — not something you claim afterwards.
  • The exact current rate, eligible battery list and taper depend on your installation date.

Indicative only, as of 24 Sep 2026. Your exact eligible rebate is confirmed as part of your quote, not guaranteed upfront by this page. Sources: Solar Choice — Cheaper Home Batteries Program changes, DCCEEW — Cheaper Home Batteries Program.
