Federal rebate

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program, explained

A federal discount applied to eligible battery systems (5-100 kWh) at the time of purchase — your installer applies it directly to your quote, not a separate claim afterwards.

Worked example — 10 kWh battery, Sydney (Zone 3)

At the current Zone 3 rate of approximately $252 per usable kWh, a 10 kWh battery (under the 14 kWh full-rate cap) works out to roughly $2,520 off the upfront cost — commonly summarised as around a 30% discount on typical system pricing.

Rate applies in full up to 14 kWh, then tapers (60% from 14-28 kWh, 15% from 28-50 kWh). The rate steps down roughly every 6 months until the scheme ends in 2030 — next change expected 1 Jan 2027. Indicative only, as of 24 Sep 2026; your exact eligible rebate depends on your installation date and is confirmed as part of your quote, not guaranteed by this page. Sizing

What size battery do I need?

A rough starting point — your actual size comes from your usage pattern and goals, confirmed during your assessment.

Small household

Lower evening usage, mainly self-consumption. Often around 5-7 kWh.

Typical family home

Evening cooking, appliances and some backup circuits. Often around 10-13 kWh.

Larger household or EV

Higher evening load, more backup circuits, or charging an EV overnight. Often 13 kWh+.

Backup vs savings

Two different benefits, not automatic together

Savings

Store solar you'd otherwise export for little value, and use it in the evening instead of buying grid power. This happens automatically once the battery's configured.

Backup

Keeps chosen circuits running during a blackout. Only available where backup circuits and changeover equipment are specifically designed and installed for it — not every battery installation includes this.

Common questions

Battery storage, answered plainly

Is a battery right for my existing solar?

It depends on how much solar you currently export unused, your evening electricity use, and your existing inverter's compatibility. A short assessment of your bill and system details usually answers this before any site visit.

What should I know about backup power?

Most battery systems don't automatically back up your whole home — backup is usually a defined set of essential circuits, set up deliberately during installation. It's worth deciding what should stay on before the system is designed.

Will a battery work with my current inverter?

Some inverters have a matching battery from the same manufacturer; others need an AC-coupled battery with its own inverter. We'll confirm what your specific system supports before quoting.
